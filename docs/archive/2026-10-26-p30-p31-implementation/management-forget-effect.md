The actual CLI caller and journal peer keep their own native owner controls. A share-capable native management journal is acquired from its first header; its Writer reserves bounded intent/result capacity before display. The retained native guardian displays the complete signed target and genuine challenge while the four descriptions are held, closes and settles every transferred copy, then waits until the Collection, Consent and Identity display callbacks have unwound before reading the same terminal. The resulting live same-owner Review permit is not spent by an intermediate observation. In that peer, the exact originally displayed intent is synchronously appended to the same journal before the existing publication helper reacquires Identity → Consent → Collection. The existing normalizer revalidates the stored root, signed target and metadata and consumes the permit once before the bounded Core operation. The existing measured accounting, consumed-review receipt, deferred settlement and journal result follow. Writer, native journal lock and Resources cleanup must also be confirmed before success.

An unconfirmed intent append does not start publication. The caller tracks the point where publication may have started separately from Review's permit state: an unknown or exception during that phase stays unknown even if the normalizer did not consume. After consumption, any failed, late or unknown completion is unknown. A known pre-effect refusal remains a refusal. Inspection never replays. The 120-second operation and 30-second host-receipt permit bounds retain the limits stated in the input review; they do not promise to recall a write already admitted before expiry.
