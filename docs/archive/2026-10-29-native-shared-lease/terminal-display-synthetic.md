# Synthetic native leased display candidate — 2026-10-29

This Linux-only, private native candidate extends the [inert matched-terminal probe](terminal-lease-inert.md) and is not a production Review or managed-output route. No runtime caller activates it; protected-output gates remain closed. The [plan](../../plan.md) owns acceptance. Its real PTY controls use synthetic native owner-control senders and four native Transaction lock owners, not actual Session/Host process attachment or the distinct management owner pair.

The candidate collects both bound EOF owner controls and all four distinct leases before forking. The reader and guardian inherit the same descriptions and retain them throughout direct writes to the verified matching tty. The guardian opens a reader pidfd and watches for reader death even before activation. A one-use activation repeats the exact bounded initial operation, owners, lease manifest, tty identity, absolute expiry and payload; a changed or repeated activation is refused. One monotonic setup/use deadline applies to the native exchange, with a separate absolute wall expiry checked before and after each readiness wait and before every bounded tty write. In-flight writes can complete after a concurrent loss; the lock descriptions remain held through writer exit. No terminal emulator consumption, human observation or retrospective recall is implied.

`L` means collection and fork, while `D` means only that the reader reported completing native writes. Neither is an effect receipt or Review success. On normal close the guardian tells the reader to stop, reaps it, drops its own descriptions, and performs authenticated per-exporter C/F/G/EOF settlement in manifest order before reporting C. Any loss, late or partial status, or incomplete closure remains unknown. The eventual host integration must bind actual Session/Host owner controls, handle all partial replies as unknown, settle and thaw only after all-copy closure, and independently revalidate Review authority. A later wall-time check cannot retrospectively invalidate bytes already queued, and kernel readiness checks do not make a syscall or filesystem preparation hard-preemptible.

The focused real-PTY results are in [the evidence directory](evidence/terminal-display-synthetic/). The final source-matched helper and fixture passed 13 modes: normal full display and closure, changed payload, changed manifest, reader death before activation without a close request, expired activation, owner loss, wrong tty, partial write followed by owner death or expiry, admitted-write/owner-loss race, repeated activation, and the inert normal and guardian-loss regressions. The admitted-write race stops the actual reader after admission, kills the guardian, Session-control sender and all original exporters, verifies that independent contenders remain blocked by the stopped reader, then resumes it. One admitted chunk can reach the tty before the reader exits; no D or C is accepted as a settled operation, and locks become available only after the reader exits. These are native write and locking observations, not full user-path qualification.

A separate real prefilled status-pipe test found that the retained v4 binary could queue D after the display wall expiry when the pipe became writable. The failed escalated log is preserved. The v6 repair checks wall time around the status readiness/write and the same test passes: the actual tty write occurred, but no late D or C was delivered and cleanup remains unknown. The status-pipe test's first sandboxed attempt failed with `frame EOF`; the successful fault reproduction and repaired control used authorized isolated PTY/Unix descriptor transfer. A helper compiled with a test-only stop hook is used solely for the partial and admitted-write controls; the production binary is independently compiled without the hook, and its hook-marker check is retained. The v4 binary and results are retained, but an exact standalone v4 source snapshot was not made; the source tar reproduces the final v6 cohort only.

The final source tar includes the native helper sources and fixture. From that snapshot, the exact compilation commands were:

```sh
rustc --edition=2021 -D warnings -O native/ipse-runner.rs -o /tmp/ipse-terminal-display-v6-helper
rustc --edition=2021 -D warnings -O --cfg ipse_terminal_display_test_hook native/ipse-runner.rs -o /tmp/ipse-terminal-display-v6-hook-helper
rustc --edition=2021 -D warnings -O dev/fixtures/terminal_lease_probe.rs -o /tmp/ipse-terminal-display-v6-fixture
```

Each mode ran as `/tmp/ipse-terminal-display-v6-fixture PRIVATE_EMPTY_DIR HELPER MODE`, with a fresh private directory. The hook helper was used only for `display-partial-owner`, `display-partial-expired`, `display-admitted-race`, and `display-replay`; the production helper was used for all other modes including `display-receipt-stall`. The logs preserve all modes, including preliminary controls and the v4 negative result. A manifest records the exact sources, binaries, platform, compiler and logs; the v4 control used the final v6 fixture with the retained earlier binary.
